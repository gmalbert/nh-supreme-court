# NH Supreme Court Judge-Prep Research Database — Project Plan

**Last revised:** July 28, 2026

## Purpose and status

Build a private, high-assurance research resource from the New Hampshire Supreme Court opinion archive. The resource should make it faster to learn governing rules, see how those rules have been applied, and trace every proposition back to the source opinion.

This is a research accelerator, not legal authority. Any proposition used for legal work must be checked against the official opinion.

The initial product is private judge-prep material. Public display may be considered much later, so the private pipeline must preserve source evidence, provenance, validation results, and review history. Public UI, publication editing, accessibility review, and public-facing disclaimers are not part of the current project.

> **Implementation warning:** The Python files in `docs/judge-prep/` are Anthropic-specific prototypes created before the repository was inspected. They do not match the real corpus and must not be used for paid bulk extraction. The decision-complete replacement specification is in [`judge-prep/phase1-bulk-extraction.md`](judge-prep/phase1-bulk-extraction.md).

## Decisions

- Process canonical authoritative opinions first. Do not treat every PDF associated with a docket as a separate precedential opinion.
- Preserve the site's existing broad topic tags and add a separate controlled, hierarchical legal-issue taxonomy for judge prep.
- Keep four analytically distinct collections: holdings, discretion calls, cited propositions, and substantive separate opinions.
- Require source paragraph anchors and locally verified short quotes for legal propositions.
- Benchmark 30 fully attorney-reviewed opinions, then adjudicate disagreements and flagged fields on 30 additional opinions.
- Compare local `mlx-community/Qwen3-14B-4bit`, DeepSeek V4 Flash, and DeepSeek V4 Pro before choosing production routing.
- Let local Qwen handle ordinary opinions only if it meets the benchmark gate. Otherwise use V4 Flash for the bulk pass. Reserve V4 Pro for difficult, failed, or quality-control records.
- Keep OpenCode Go paid-balance fallback disabled during benchmarking and pilot runs.
- Keep judge-prep implementation material and generated results under the Git-ignored `docs/judge-prep/` area. Maintain a separate private backup because Git will not preserve these files.

## Repository facts that control the design

The canonical inputs are:

- `data/processed/all_opinions.json` for metadata.
- `data/processed/text/*.txt` for extracted opinion text.
- `data/topic_taxonomy.json` for the existing broad site taxonomy.
- `utils/citations.py` and `scripts/build_citation_index.py` for the current deterministic citation work.

The archive currently contains 2,804 metadata records and 2,768 unique `case_number` values. It is not a clean one-record-per-case corpus:

- There are 36 additional records sharing a `case_number` with another record.
- Duplicate-docket groups include revised opinions, original opinions, modification orders, summaries, and some distinct decisions.
- `source_file_key` is also not globally unique; `pdf_url` is unique across the current 2,804 records.
- All records can presently resolve some text through `case_number`, but 178 lack a text file named for their `source_file_key`. Blind fallback can attach a full opinion's text to an ancillary order or summary.
- Eight resolved text files contain fewer than 100 bytes and 23 contain fewer than 1,000 bytes.
- Ten documents exceed roughly 100,000 characters; these require an explicit long-document strategy.
- `citation_edges.csv` is sparse and contains apparent self-citations. It is not reliable ground truth and must be audited or rebuilt before citation coverage is used for validation.

The full corpus contains about 56.7 million characters or 9.05 million whitespace-delimited words. A one-pass extraction is estimated at 15.8–18.0 million input tokens plus roughly 4.6–5.3 million output tokens. These figures are planning estimates; actual provider usage must be recorded per request.

## Research record

Source metadata must be merged from the corpus, not regenerated by a model. Each canonical opinion record should contain:

- Stable source-document ID, case number, source file key, PDF URL, case name, citation, dates, lower court, and corpus opinion type.
- Source-text hash, canonical-version decision, and any relationship to an original, revised, or modification document.
- Broad site topics copied from existing metadata.
- Controlled judge-prep issue tags, plus separately quarantined proposals for new tags.
- Procedural posture, disposition, and a short factual and legal summary.
- Portable holdings of the court, kept distinct from fact-bound applications of discretionary standards.
- Cited cases and the propositions for which the opinion used them, including treatment such as applied, quoted, distinguished, or rejected.
- Substantive concurrences and dissents, clearly attributed and never mixed with majority holdings.
- Global paragraph anchors and a short exact quote supporting every holding and cited proposition.
- Provider, exact model, prompt version, schema version, run ID, timestamp, token usage, latency, validation status, and review history.
- Structured review reasons instead of relying only on a model's self-reported confidence.

Authority status must be derived from corpus evidence and explicit court language. A model must not decide that a document is precedential merely from its length, signature, or formatting.

## Taxonomy

The existing broad tags remain unchanged so current pages and analysis continue to work. Judge-prep extraction adds a separate controlled taxonomy using stable IDs and human-readable paths such as:

- `Constitutional Law > Due Process > Notice Before Taking`
- `Civil Procedure > Standard of Review > Summary Judgment`
- `Evidence > Rule 403 > Unfair Prejudice`
- `Family Law > Divorce > Property Division`
- `Torts > Medical Malpractice > Proximate Cause`

The initial issue taxonomy should be seeded from the reviewed samples and expanded during the benchmark. A model may propose an unknown issue, but the proposal must enter a taxonomy-review queue and cannot silently become a production tag.

## Delivery phases

### Phase 0 — Corpus inventory and benchmark

1. Build a canonical manifest and a separate exception queue for ambiguous versions, ancillary documents, missing text, short text, and malformed metadata.
2. Audit deterministic citation extraction and rebuild the citation index if necessary.
3. Number normalized opinion paragraphs globally so every extracted proposition can cite stable source locations.
4. Select 30 diverse opinions for full attorney annotation and 30 more for targeted adjudication. Cover eras, practice areas, lengths, mixed dispositions, separate opinions, discretionary rulings, and degraded text.
5. Run identical versioned prompts through local Qwen, V4 Flash, and V4 Pro. Score results blindly before selecting production routing.

The nine records in `docs/case_digest_samples.json` are useful candidate examples, not a validated benchmark. They were not produced through a comparative, scored evaluation and must be re-reviewed before inclusion in the gold set.

### Phase 1 — Case-level extraction

Process one canonical opinion per request using the strict provider-neutral contract in the Phase 1 specification. Write one resumable artifact per document, validate it locally, and escalate only failures or high-risk records.

Roll out in gates:

1. Benchmark only.
2. Twenty-five-record production pilot.
3. One hundred records.
4. Five hundred records.
5. Remaining canonical corpus after quality, cost, and recovery checks pass.

### Phase 2 — Compilation and review

Compile only validated per-document artifacts into case digests, cited propositions, discretion calls, separate opinions, and review queues. Compilation must be deterministic and idempotent; rerunning it cannot duplicate citations or preserve stale failures.

Require human review for unresolved validation failures, ambiguous canonical versions, taxonomy proposals, separate-opinion attribution problems, unsupported propositions, and a random five-percent quality-control sample.

### Phase 3 — Topic synthesis

After a topic's case-level records are sufficiently complete and reviewed, synthesize a private topic brief from the validated holdings in chronological order. Keep black-letter rules separate from illustrative discretion calls, identify later treatment and tension, and link every statement back to case-level evidence.

Topic briefs are model-assisted drafts and receive their own source and review checks. They must not claim that the most frequently cited case is controlling merely because it is frequent.

### Phase 4 — Optional private interface

Add private browsing or search only after the underlying records meet the quality gates. JSON remains an acceptable interchange format; SQLite may be added later if private query performance or relational integrity justifies it.

### Phase 5 — Possible public release

Public release is a separate future project. It would require editorial review, accessibility work, clear machine-assistance labels, publication policy, stronger regression testing, and a fresh legal and product review. Nothing in the current plan authorizes automatic publication.

## Model and cost strategy

### Local challenger

Use `mlx-community/Qwen3-14B-4bit` through an MLX OpenAI-compatible local endpoint. Begin in non-thinking mode for predictable structured output and practical throughput. Test thinking mode only on a small difficult-case subset if the initial benchmark shows a meaningful quality gap.

Local Qwen qualifies for ordinary-opinion routing only if it produces no material unsupported legal statements in the gold set, reaches at least 90% holding precision, 85% holding recall, and 95% disposition accuracy, and trails Flash by no more than five percentage points on the agreed aggregate score.

### OpenCode Go

- Flash model ID: `deepseek-v4-flash`
- Pro model ID: `deepseek-v4-pro`
- Endpoint: `https://opencode.ai/zen/go/v1/chat/completions`

Provider reference: [OpenCode Go documentation](https://opencode.ai/docs/go/). Local model reference: [MLX Community Qwen3-14B 4-bit model card](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/Qwen3-14B-4bit).

At rates published when this plan was revised, a Flash pass over the full corpus would consume roughly $3.50–$4.00 of allowance; Pro across the full corpus would consume roughly $11–$13. The preferred local/Flash/Pro escalation path is expected to consume about $5–$7 of allowance, depending on benchmark results and escalation volume. Provider prices and limits are time-sensitive and must be verified immediately before execution.

Do not automatically fall back from subscription allowance to paid Zen balance. Do not silently switch providers when a request fails. A provider change creates a new recorded attempt.

## Quality gates

- One hundred percent of accepted records pass strict schema validation.
- Every stored quote is an exact substring of the normalized source and appears in the cited paragraph.
- Source metadata and stable IDs match the canonical manifest exactly.
- No majority holding is sourced solely from a concurrence or dissent.
- Citation propositions correspond to deterministic citation candidates or enter human review.
- Interrupted runs resume without resubmitting completed records.
- Recompilation is idempotent.
- Benchmark and pilot reports state field-level accuracy, material-error counts, token usage, cost-equivalent usage, latency, failures, and escalation rates.
