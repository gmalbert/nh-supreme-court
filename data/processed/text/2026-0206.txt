THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2026-0206, A.A. v. R.W., the court on May 26,
2026, issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The plaintiff appeals an order of the Circuit Court (Burns, J.)
dismissing a civil stalking petition, without a hearing, on the basis that the
“[p]etition does not meet the standard for relief set forth in RSA 633:3-a.” See
RSA 633:3-a, III-a (2016). We construe the trial court’s order as having
determined that the petition failed to state a claim upon which relief may be
granted, see In the Matter of Salesky & Salesky, 157 N.H. 698, 702 (2008)
(stating that interpretation of a trial court order is a question of law subject to de
novo review), and we affirm.
In reviewing the trial court’s decision to dismiss the plaintiff’s petition for
failure to state a claim upon which relief may be granted, we assume the well-
pleaded facts in the petition to be true and construe all reasonable inferences
from those facts in the light most favorable to the plaintiff. See New England
Backflow v. Gagne, 172 N.H. 655, 661 (2019); In the Matter of Sawyer &
Sawyer, 161 N.H. 11, 14 (2010). “We then engage in a threshold inquiry that
tests the facts in the petition against the applicable law.” Sawyer, 161 N.H. at
14 (quotation omitted). We will uphold the trial court’s decision if the pleaded
facts do not constitute a basis for legal relief. Gagne, 172 N.H. at 661.
It is the burden of the appealing party, in this case the plaintiff, to
provide a record on appeal that is sufficient to decide the issues being raised.
See Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250 (2004). This requirement
applies to relevant trial court pleadings, such as the plaintiff’s civil stalking
petition, see Estate of Day v. Hanover Ins. Co., 162 N.H. 415, 422 (2011)
(applying Bean to appealing party’s failure to provide relevant pleadings filed in
the trial court); Sup. Ct. R. 13(1) (defining the “record” to include “[t]he papers
and exhibits filed and considered in the proceedings in the trial court”), and is
not relaxed for self-represented parties, see In the Matter of Birmingham &
Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51, 56 (2006). We assume that relevant portions of the
trial court record not provided on appeal support the result reached by the trial
court. See Estate of Day, 162 N.H. at 422; Bean, 151 N.H. at 250.
In this case, the plaintiff has not provided the civil stalking petition filed
in the trial court. Accordingly, we assume that the petition supports the trial
court’s determination that it did “not meet the standard for relief set forth in
RSA 633:3-a,” see Estate of Day, 162 N.H. at 422; Bean, 151 N.H. at 250, and
we uphold the trial court’s decision to dismiss the petition for failure to state a
claim upon which relief may be granted under RSA 633:3-a, III-a.
In light of this order, the defendant’s motion to dismiss and response to
the plaintiff’s allegations is moot.
Affirmed.
Donovan, Countway, Gould, and Will, JJ., concurred.
