THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2026-0258, G.W. v. S.J., the court on July 17,
2026, issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted on
appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See Sup. Ct.
R. 20(3). The defendant appeals an order of the Circuit Court (Bernazzani, J.),
issued following a hearing, granting a civil stalking final order of protection in
favor of the plaintiff. See RSA 633:3-a (Supp. 2025). On appeal, the defendant
challenges the trial court’s credibility determinations and factual findings. We
uphold such findings unless they are lacking in evidentiary support or are
erroneous as a matter of law, and we accord considerable weight to the court’s
judgments on the credibility of witnesses and the weight to be given testimony.
See Fisher v. Minichiello, 155 N.H. 188, 190 (2007). Moreover, “[i]t is the burden
of the appealing party . . . to provide this court with a record sufficient to decide
[his] issues on appeal.” Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250 (2004).
Here, the defendant did not provide a transcript of the hearing before the trial
court. Absent a transcript of the hearing, “we must assume that the evidence
was sufficient to support the result reached by the trial court.” Id.; In the Matter
of Birmingham & Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51, 56-57 (2006) (noting that procedural
rules are not relaxed for self-represented parties).
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Donovan, Countway, Gould, and Will, JJ.,
