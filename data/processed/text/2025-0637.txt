THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2025-0637, State of New Hampshire v. Lisa
Marie Michel, the court on June 1, 2026, issued the following
order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The defendant, Lisa Marie Michel, appeals her convictions in
the Circuit Court (Gorman, J.), following a hearing, of misdemeanor aggravated
driving while having an alcohol concentration of 0.16 or more. See RSA 265-
A:3, III (2024). We affirm.
The defendant, as the appealing party, has the burden to provide this
court with a sufficient record to decide her issues on appeal and to
demonstrate that she raised her issues in the trial court. State v. Brooks, 162
N.H. 570, 583 (2011); see also State v. Porter, 144 N.H. 96, 100-01 (1999) (self-
represented litigants are bound by the same procedural rules that govern
parties represented by counsel). In addition, we will not review issues without
developed legal argument and supporting authority. State v. Durgin, 165 N.H.
725, 731 (2013).
The defendant has not provided a record sufficient to decide her issues or
to demonstrate that she raised her issues in the trial court. See Brooks, 162
N.H. at 583. She has not provided a transcript of the trial or copies of any
exhibits that may have been introduced at trial. Nor did she timely file a
motion requesting this court to order the trial court to transmit any exhibits.
See Sup. Ct. R. 13(4). Without an adequate record, we cannot evaluate the
defendant’s claims regarding the use of video evidence at trial or review the
trial court’s evidentiary rulings at trial. We must assume that the evidence was
sufficient to support the trial court’s decision. See Atwood v. Owens, 142 N.H.
396, 396 (1997).
In addition, the defendant’s brief does not provide developed legal
argument or legal authority to support her position that the State’s method of
complying with its discovery obligations was inadequate or that it otherwise
violated the defendant’s right to due process. See Durgin, 165 N.H. at 731.
Affirmed.
Donovan, Countway, and Gould, JJ., concurred.
