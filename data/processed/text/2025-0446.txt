THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2025-0446, State of New Hampshire v. Grace
Woodham, the court on July 10, 2026, issued the following
order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The defendant, Grace Woodham, appeals an order of the
Superior Court (Frizzell, J.) denying a motion to reconsider an earlier order that
had denied a motion seeking the return of certain property seized pursuant to
a warrant. We affirm.
As the appealing party, the defendant has the burden of demonstrating
reversible error. Gallo v. Traina, 166 N.H. 737, 740 (2014). It is also the
defendant’s burden to provide those portions of the trial court record that are
necessary to decide the issues she raises on appeal, and we assume that
relevant portions of the trial court record not provided on appeal support the
trial court’s decision. See Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250
(2004). This requirement applies to relevant trial court pleadings. See Estate
of Day v. Hanover Ins. Co. 162 N.H. 415, 422 (2011).
In this case, the trial court denied the defendant’s motion to reconsider
“for [the] reasons in [the] State’s objection” to the motion. The defendant has
not provided a copy of the State’s objection to the defendant’s motion to
reconsider as part of the record on appeal. Based on our review of the trial
court order denying the motion to reconsider, the defendant’s arguments, the
relevant law, and the portions of the record provided on appeal, we conclude
that the defendant has failed to demonstrate that the trial court erred by
denying the motion to reconsider. See Gallo, 166 N.H. at 740; see also Sup. Ct.
R. 25(8).
Affirmed.
Donovan, Countway, Gould, and Will, JJ., concurred.
