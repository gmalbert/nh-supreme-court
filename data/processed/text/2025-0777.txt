THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2025-0777, Tony Olivolo v. Nicole McNeal, the
court on June 2, 2026, issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted on
appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See Sup. Ct.
R. 20(3). The plaintiff, Tony Olivolo, appeals an order of the Circuit Court (Ryan,
J.), issued following a hearing, entering judgment in favor of the defendant,
Nicole McNeal, in a small claims action to recover the purchase price he had paid
to the defendant for a puppy. On appeal, the plaintiff challenges the trial court’s
order. We uphold the trial court’s findings unless they are lacking in evidentiary
support or are erroneous as a matter of law, and we defer to the court’s
judgments on the credibility of witnesses and the weight to be given testimony.
See Short v. LaPlante, Trs., 174 N.H. 384, 387 (2021); Durgin v. Pillsbury Lake
Water Dist., 153 N.H. 818, 821 (2006). Moreover, “[i]t is the burden of the
appealing party . . . to provide this court with a record sufficient to decide [his]
issues on appeal.” Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250 (2004).
Here, the plaintiff did not provide a transcript of the hearing before the trial
court. Accordingly, absent a transcript of the hearing, “we must assume that the
evidence was sufficient to support the result reached by the trial court.” Id.; In
the Matter of Birmingham & Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51, 56-57 (2006) (noting that
procedural rules are not relaxed for self-represented parties). Therefore, we
affirm the trial court’s order.
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Donovan, Countway, Gould, and Will, JJ.,
