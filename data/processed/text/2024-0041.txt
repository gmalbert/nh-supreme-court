THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2024-0041, Merrimack Valley Roofing, LLC v.
Christopher Mastorakis, the court on November 12, 2024, issued
the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(2). The defendant, Christopher Mastorakis, appeals a decision
of the Circuit Court (Zaino, J.), following a final hearing on the merits,
awarding the plaintiff, Merrimack Valley Roofing, LLC, $5,995 on its small
claim for nonpayment of a contract to install a roof on the defendant’s home,
and denying the defendant’s counterclaim for costs incurred in replacing a
portion of the roof. We construe the defendant’s brief to be arguing that the
evidence was not sufficient to support the trial court’s decision. We affirm.
Whether a party has materially breached a contract is a question of fact
for the trial court to resolve. See Fitz v. Coutinho, 136 N.H. 721, 725 (1993).
Likewise, “[w]hen there is a disputed question of fact as to the existence and
terms of a contract, [the question] is to be determined by the trier of fact.”
Syncom Indus. v. Wood, 155 N.H. 73, 82 (2007). We will uphold the trial
court’s findings and conclusions unless they are lacking in evidentiary support
or are tainted by error of law. Id. We defer to the trial court’s judgment on
issues such as resolving conflicts in testimony, evaluating the credibility of
witnesses, and determining the weight to be given to the evidence presented,
mindful that the trial court is not compelled to believe even uncontested
evidence. See Gaucher v. Waterhouse, 175 N.H. 291, 296 (2022); Loon Valley
Homeowner’s Ass’n v. Pollock, 171 N.H. 75, 78 (2018). “We do not decide
whether we would have ruled differently than the trial court, but, rather,
whether a reasonable person could have reached the same decision based
upon the same evidence.” Gaucher, 175 N.H. at 295-96.
It is the burden of the appealing party, in this case the defendant, to
provide a record on appeal that is sufficient to decide the issues being raised,
and to demonstrate that those issues were raised in the trial court. See Bean
v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250 (2004). Absent a transcript of an
evidentiary hearing, we assume that the evidence was sufficient to support the
result reached by the trial court, and we review its decision for errors of law
appearing on the face of its order only. See id.; Atwood v. Owens, 142 N.H.
396, 396-97 (1997); Sup. Ct. R. 15(3) (“If the [appealing] party intends to argue
in the supreme court that a finding or conclusion is unsupported by the
evidence or is contrary to the evidence, the [appealing] party shall include in
the record a transcript of all evidence relevant to such finding or conclusion.”)
These rules are not relaxed for self-represented parties. See In the Matter of
Birmingham & Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51, 56-57 (2006).
In this case, the defendant has not provided a transcript of the hearing
on the merits held in this case. Accordingly, we assume that the evidence
supports the trial court’s findings that the plaintiff complied with the terms of
the parties’ contract and that the defendant breached it, and finding no error of
law on the face of the trial court’s order, we uphold its decision.
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Bassett, Donovan, and Countway, JJ., concurred.
