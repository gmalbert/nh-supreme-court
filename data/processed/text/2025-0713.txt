THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2025-0713, In the Matter of J.B. and K.G., the
court on May 27, 2026, issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The father of I.B. (child) appeals the order of the Circuit
Court (Christie, J.), following a hearing, entering a final parenting plan. The
father argues that the court erred by: (1) making insufficient findings;
(2) finding that he committed domestic violence; (3) depriving him of his right to
discovery and right to due process; and (4) failing to properly apply the
statutory best interest factors. We affirm.
The father failed to appear at the September 30, 2025 final hearing on
the parenting plan. According to the trial court’s order, the mother testified by
offer of proof that she obtained a restraining order against the father after he
committed an act of domestic violence against her. The court found that the
father had abused the mother for purposes of RSA chapter 173-B, and,
therefore, awarded her sole decision-making responsibility for the child. See
RSA 461-A:6, I(j) (Supp. 2025) (providing that, in determining parental rights
and responsibilities, the court shall consider, among other factors, any
evidence of abuse as defined in RSA 173-B:1, I, and the impact of the abuse on
the child and on the relationship between the child and the abusing parent).
The court noted that, on October 30, 2024, the father appeared before the
court with a financial affidavit claiming monthly income of $4,000. Although
the father asserts that he is now unemployed, the court found that the mother
“credibly testified she believes he is still employed,” noting that the father is
often dressed in his work attire during video visits with the child. The court
imputed income to the father of $4,000 per month and ordered guidelines-level
support of $737 per month.
The father argues that the court erred by: (1) making insufficient findings
under RSA 461-A:11 (2018) to support its parenting plan;1 (2) finding the
evidence sufficient to prove that he committed domestic violence against the
mother; (3) depriving him of his right to discovery and right to due process; and
(4) failing to properly apply the best interest factors set forth in RSA 461-A:6
(2018 & Supp. 2025).
1Although the record is insufficient to address this issue on the merits, we note that RSA 461-
A:11 applies to orders modifying permanent parenting plans, see In the Matter of Kurowski &
Kurowski, 161 N.H. 578, 586 (2011), which is not the situation here.
It is a long-standing rule that parties may not have judicial review of
matters not raised in the trial court. Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H.
248, 250 (2004); see also In the Matter of Birmingham & Birmingham, 154
N.H. 51, 56 (2006) (self-represented litigants are bound by the same procedural
rules that govern parties represented by counsel). Furthermore, it is the
burden of the appealing party, here the father, to provide this court with a
record sufficient to decide the issues on appeal and to demonstrate that he
raised those issues in the trial court. Id. The father failed to provide this court
with a transcript of the final hearing on the parenting plan. See Sup. Ct. R.
15(2)(a) (establishing procedure for ordering transcripts in notice of appeal
form). The limited record provided on appeal fails to show that the father
raised his issues in the trial court. See Bean, 151 N.H. at 250. Additionally,
without a transcript, we must assume that the evidence was sufficient to
support the trial court’s ruling. See Atwood v. Owens, 142 N.H. 396, 396
(1997). We review the court’s order for errors of law only, see id. at 397, and
find none.
Affirmed.
