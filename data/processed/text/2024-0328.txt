THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2024-0328, Elizabeth Lacount & a. v. KAB
Realty Management, LLC, the court on June 2, 2025, issued the
following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The defendant, KAB Realty Management, LLC (landlord),
appeals an order of the Circuit Court (Gardner, J.), following a final hearing,
awarding the plaintiffs, Elizabeth Lacount and Cotrell Trotter, $1,000 for a
violation of the right to quiet enjoyment of a residential tenancy, and $1,000
per day for each of the four days that the violation continued after the issuance
of a temporary order. See RSA 540-A:2 (2021), :4, VII(b), IX(a) (2021) (amended
2024). The landlord argues that the evidence was insufficient to support
findings that it acted “willfully” and failed to comply with the temporary order.
We affirm.
RSA 540-A:2 prohibits a landlord from “willfully violat[ing] a tenant’s
right to quiet enjoyment of [a] tenancy.” RSA 540-A:2 protects a tenant’s right
to quiet enjoyment, a common law doctrine that obligates a landlord to refrain
from interfering with a tenant’s right to possession of leased property during
the tenancy. Magee v. Cooper, 174 N.H. 647, 650 (2021). The right of quiet
enjoyment is violated when a landlord substantially interferes with the tenant’s
beneficial use or enjoyment of the leased property. Id. To constitute a violation
of RSA 540-A:2 and trigger the damages provisions of RSA 540-A:4, however,
the violation must be “willful.” Id. at 650-51. The term “willfully” “denotes a
voluntary and intentional act,” not a “mistaken or accidental act.” Id. at 651
(quotation omitted).
Upon proof of a willful breach of the covenant of quiet enjoyment, the
trial court may award damages to the plaintiff. RSA 540-A:4, VII(b). “Any
landlord who violates RSA 540-A:2 . . . shall be subject to the civil remedies set
forth in RSA 358-A:10 for the initial violation . . . . Each day that a violation
continues after issuance of a temporary order shall constitute a separate
violation.” RSA 540-A:4, IX(a). Accordingly, an award of damages under RSA
540-A:4, IX(a) may include $1,000 for the initial violation of RSA 540-A:2, and
an additional $1,000 per day for each day that the violation continues after the
issuance of a temporary order under RSA 540-A:4, VIII. See Randall v.
Abounaja, 164 N.H. 506, 509-11 (2013); RSA 358-A:10, I (2022).
Whether a landlord has substantially interfered with a tenant’s right to
quiet enjoyment or has acted willfully are questions of fact. Magee, 174 N.H. at
651. The trial court’s “findings of fact shall be final but questions of law may
be transferred to the supreme court in the same manner as from the superior
court.” RSA 540-A:4, V. “By the statute’s plain terms, review is limited and, as
to factual findings, it is highly deferential.” Magee, 174 N.H. at 650. We will
uphold the trial court’s findings and rulings unless they lack evidentiary
support or are otherwise tainted by error of law. Id. at 651. “Our inquiry is to
determine whether the evidence presented to the trial court reasonably
supports its findings, and then whether the court’s decision is consonant with
applicable law.” Id. (quotation and brackets omitted).
In this case, there was evidence establishing that in late December 2023,
the landlord was notified of a leak in the apartment occupied by the plaintiffs
coming from the structure’s roof. According to the plaintiffs, the leak was
substantial, causing saturated sections of ceiling and of walls to fall and create
a safety risk, including in a bedroom occupied by children, and causing mold
to form. There was further testimony that the landlord ignored the plaintiffs’
emails and multiple verbal requests to address the matter, and did not address
the matter until the week of February 19, 2024, after the plaintiffs had filed the
present petition, and after the trial court issued a temporary order on February
12 requiring the landlord to “repair the leaky ceiling and walls that have been
falling.” We note that according to the landlord’s own property manager, the
landlord received the temporary order on February 15, four days prior to the
week of February 19, when, the property manager testified, the maintenance
staff repaired the leaking ceiling and walls. We note further that at trial, the
parties introduced exhibits, including photographs of the damages and email
correspondence. The defendant has not, however, provided the exhibits as part
of the record on appeal. Accordingly, we assume that the exhibits support the
trial court’s decision. See Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250
(2004) (stating that appealing party bears burden to provide those parts of the
trial court record that are necessary to decide issues on appeal, and that
relevant portions of the record not provided are assumed to support the trial
court’s decision); see also In the Matter of Rokowski & Rokowski, 168 N.H. 57,
62 (2015) (applying Bean to appealing party’s failure to provide exhibits
admitted into evidence at evidentiary hearing).
Although the landlord’s property manager may have disputed the
plaintiffs’ testimony, we defer to the trial court’s determinations regarding the
weight of the evidence and the credibility of the witnesses. See Cook v.
Sullivan, 149 N.H. 774, 780 (2003). Upon this record, the trial court’s findings
that the landlord willfully violated the right of quiet enjoyment and that the
violation continued at least four days after the temporary order issued were
2
neither lacking in evidentiary support nor tainted by error of law. Magee, 174
N.H. at 651.
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Bassett, Donovan, and Countway, JJ., concurred.
