THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2025-0453, Kevin F. MacMillan v. Town of
Conway, the court on July 21, 2026, issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The plaintiff, Kevin F. MacMillan, appeals an order of the
Superior Court (Klass, J.), arguing that the trial court erred in dismissing, on
res judicata grounds, his complaint regarding a cell tower permit issued by the
defendant, the Town of Conway. We affirm.
The trial court found that the plaintiff’s complaint involved the permitting
of the same cell tower that was at issue in a prior planning board appeal to the
superior court — in which the plaintiff intervened — and in a zoning board
appeal brought by the plaintiff. In the zoning board appeal, the plaintiff
appealed the superior court’s decision upholding the zoning board’s decision,
and we affirmed. See Kevin F. MacMillan v. Town of Conway, No. 2022-0489
(non-precedential order), 2023 WL 2825935 (N.H. March 22, 2023). In this
case, the trial court found that res judicata bars the plaintiff’s complaint
because the parties are the same as in the prior appeals, the prior appeals
resulted in final judgments on the merits, and the causes of action in this
appeal are the same as in the plaintiff’s prior appeals. See Walker v. Day, 173
N.H. 211, 213 (2020) (“The doctrine of res judicata precludes the litigation in a
later case of matters actually decided, and matters that could have been
litigated, in an earlier action between the same parties for the same cause of
action.”).
It is the plaintiff’s burden as the appellant to provide an adequate record
and to demonstrate that the trial court erred. Coyle v. Battles, 147 N.H. 98,
100 (2001); see also In the Matter of Birmingham & Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51,
56 (2006) (self-represented litigants are bound by the same procedural rules
that govern parties represented by counsel). The plaintiff has not provided us
with the complaint that the trial court dismissed and which forms the basis for
this appeal. Nor has he presented developed legal arguments regarding the
issues he raises on appeal. See State v. Blackmer, 149 N.H. 47, 49 (2003) (“[I]n
the realm of appellate review, a mere laundry list of complaints regarding
adverse rulings by the trial court, without developed legal argument, is
insufficient to warrant judicial review.” (quotation omitted)). We conclude that
the plaintiff has not met his burden to provide an adequate record or to
demonstrate that the trial court erred. See Coyle, 147 N.H. at 100.
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Donovan, Countway, Gould, and Will, JJ.,
concurred.
