THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2026-0227, N.W. v. T.S., the court on August
31, 2026, issued the following order:
The plaintiff’s motions for relief from judgment are denied.
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The plaintiff, N.W., appeals the order of the Circuit Court
(Hall, J.), following a hearing, finding that she has not been stalked by the
defendant, T.S. See RSA 633:3-a (2016). We construe her brief to argue that
the trial court’s decision was contrary to the evidence and that the judge was
biased against her. We affirm.
It is the burden of the appealing party, here the plaintiff, to provide this
court with a record sufficient to decide her issues on appeal. See Bean v. Red
Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250 (2004); see also In the Matter of
Birmingham & Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51, 56 (2006) (self-represented litigants
are bound by the same procedural rules that govern parties represented by
counsel). The plaintiff did not provide a transcript of the March 16, 2026 final
hearing on her stalking petition. See Sup. Ct. R. 15(3) (“If the moving party
intends to argue in the supreme court that a finding or conclusion is
unsupported by the evidence or is contrary to the evidence, the moving party
shall include in the record a transcript of all evidence relevant to such finding
or conclusion.”). Absent a transcript of the hearing, we must assume that the
evidence was sufficient to support the court’s decision. See Atwood v. Owens,
142 N.H. 396, 396 (1997).
Contrary to the plaintiff’s assertion, the court’s issuance of a temporary
protective order in this case did not relieve her of the burden to prove stalking,
by a preponderance of the evidence, at the final hearing. See RSA 173-B:4, I
(2022) (setting forth procedures for temporary relief); RSA 633:3-a, III-a
(incorporating procedures set forth in RSA chapter 173-B).
Finally, based upon the limited record provided for our review, we
conclude that the plaintiff has failed to demonstrate that the trial judge was
biased against her. See Sup. Ct. R. 38, Canon 2.11; State v. Bader, 148 N.H.
265, 271 (2002) (noting that adverse judicial rulings alone do not render a
judge biased).
Affirmed.
Donovan, Countway, and Will, JJ., concurred.
