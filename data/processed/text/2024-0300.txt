THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2024-0300, Kyle Holden v. Matthew Ping DBA
Ledgeview Commercial Partners, the court on January 23, 2025,
issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted on
appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See Sup. Ct.
R. 20(2). The defendant, Matthew Ping, DBA Ledgeview Commercial Partners,
appeals an order of the Circuit Court (Bedrick, J.) entering judgment in favor of
the plaintiff, Kyle Holden, in a small claims action to recover a portion of his
security deposit. The defendant argues that the trial court erred in finding that
the security deposit was not “unclaimed” by the plaintiff within the meaning of
RSA 540-A:8, II (2021). As the appealing party, the defendant has the burden of
demonstrating reversible error. Gallo v. Traina, 166 N.H. 737, 740 (2014). The
defendant also has the burden of providing this court with a record sufficient to
decide the issues raised on appeal. Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248,
250 (2004) (holding that, absent a transcript of the hearing, we must assume
that the evidence was sufficient to support the result reached by the trial court);
In the Matter of Birmingham & Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51, 56-57 (2006) (noting
that such rules are not relaxed for self-represented parties). Based upon our
review of the trial court’s order, the defendant’s challenges to it, the relevant law,
and the record submitted on appeal — which does not include a transcript of the
hearing — we conclude that the defendant has not demonstrated reversible error
and affirm the court’s decision. See Gallo, 166 N.H. at 740; Sup. Ct. R. 25(8).
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Bassett, Donovan, and Countway, JJ., concurred.
