THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2026-0049, V.C. v. W.C., the court on August
14, 2026, issued the following order:
The defendant’s motion to transfer the record is denied. See Sup. Ct. R.
13(4). The defendant’s motion to supplement the record with a table of
contents to his appendix is granted. The defendant’s motion for leave to reply
to the plaintiff’s objection to his motion for an extension of time is moot; the
court granted the defendant’s motion for an extension, in part, on June 15,
2026. The defendant’s motion to strike a portion of the same objection for
lacking evidentiary support is denied. The defendant’s “motion for leave to
proceed on the original record” is denied. The defendant’s motion for leave to
file an (untimely) reply brief is denied.
The plaintiff’s motion to seal is denied.
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The defendant, W.C., appeals the order of the Circuit Court
(Paine, J.), following a hearing, extending domestic violence and civil stalking
protective orders for the protection of the plaintiff, V.C., for one year, until
September 9, 2026. See RSA 173-B:5, VI (2022); RSA 633:3-a, II-a (2016).
We affirm.
We observe that the defendant’s complaint relates to the court’s
temporary orders issued in 2024, not to the extension order. It is the burden
of the appealing party, here the defendant, to provide this court with a record
sufficient to decide his issues on appeal, and to demonstrate that he raised his
issues in the trial court. Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250
(2004); see also In the Matter of Birmingham & Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51, 56
(2006) (self-represented litigants are bound by the same procedural rules that
govern parties represented by counsel). Even assuming, without deciding, that
the defendant did not waive his personal jurisdiction challenge by participating
in the final domestic violence and civil stalking hearings, but see In re J.M.,
177 N.H. 142, 145 (2024), 2024 N.H. 70, ¶6 (defendant waives jurisdictional
challenge by proceeding on the merits), the defendant has failed to provide a
record to demonstrate that he raised this issue in the trial court.
To the extent that the defendant’s brief may be construed to raise
additional issues, we conclude that they are not sufficiently developed, see
State v. Blackmer, 149 N.H. 47, 48 (2003), or otherwise do not warrant further
discussion, see Vogel v. Vogel, 137 N.H. 321, 322 (1993).
Affirmed.
Donovan, Gould, and Will, JJ., concurred.
