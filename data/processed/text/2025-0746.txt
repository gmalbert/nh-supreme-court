THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2025-0746, State of New Hampshire v. Robert
Harris, the court on July 13, 2026, issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted on
appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See Sup. Ct.
R. 20(3). The defendant, Robert Harris, appeals his conviction, following a bench
trial in Circuit Court (Tessier, J.), on one misdemeanor count of driving after
revocation or suspension. See RSA 263:64, VI (Supp. 2025). On appeal, the
defendant argues that his conviction should be reversed because: (1) the traffic
stop which led to his conviction was unconstitutional; (2) the evidence was
insufficient to support his conviction; and (3) the trial in the circuit court was
fundamentally unfair and infringed upon his right to due process of law.
However, “[i]t is a long-standing rule that parties may not have judicial review of
matters not raised in the forum of trial.” Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H.
248, 250 (2004). “It is the burden of the appealing party . . . to provide this court
with a record sufficient to decide [his] issues on appeal, as well as to demonstrate
that [he] raised [his] issues before the trial court.” Id. In this case, the defendant
did not provide a transcript of the trial in the circuit court. Thus, the defendant
has neither demonstrated that he preserved his arguments for our review, nor
carried his burden to provide us with a record sufficient to decide them. Indeed,
without a transcript of the trial, “we must assume that the evidence was
sufficient to support the result reached by the trial court.” Id.; see also In the
Matter of Birmingham & Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51, 56-57 (2006) (noting that
procedural rules are not relaxed for self-represented parties). Accordingly, we
affirm the defendant’s conviction.
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Donovan, Countway, Gould, and Will, JJ.,
concurred.
