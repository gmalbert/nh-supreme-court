THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2024-0085, State of New Hampshire v.
Christopher Dennis Mackey, the court on December 20, 2024,
issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the limited record
submitted on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this
order. See Sup. Ct. R. 20(2). Following a jury trial in the Superior Court
(Schulman, J.), the defendant, Christopher Mackey, was convicted on one
count of disobeying a police officer. See RSA 265:4 (2024). The complaint
alleged that he “purposely neglected to stop when signaled to do so by [police
officers] . . . by means of audible and visual emergency warning signals
equipped on their fully marked Salem police cruisers.” Following his
conviction, the defendant filed this appeal in which he seeks review of several
issues, including: (1) whether the Trial Court (St. Hilaire, J.) erred in denying
his motion for an interlocutory appeal; (2) whether the Trial Court (Schulman,
J.) erred in denying his request to present the defenses of entrapment, duress
and competing harms; and (3) whether the trial court erred in making several
evidentiary rulings that limited his proposed presentation of his case. We
affirm.
As the appealing party, the defendant has the burden of providing us with
a record sufficient both to decide the issues that he raises on appeal as well as to
demonstrate that he raised his issues before the trial court. Bean v. Red Oak
Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250 (2004). The purpose of this requirement is to
apprise the opposing party and this court of the issues presented on appeal as
well as to insure that the record is sufficiently developed to support a decision on
the merits. State v. Thiel, 160 N.H. 462, 464 (2010). The issues raised by the
defendant challenge rulings made by the trial court and require a record of the
relevant trial court proceedings to enable appellate review. See RSA 491:17
(2010) (setting forth scope of issues that may be transferred in the discretion of
the presiding justice); Piane v. Town of Conway, 118 N.H. 883, 884 (1978)
(interlocutory appeals in criminal cases are limited both to exceptional
circumstances and to the discretion of the trial court); RSA 626:7 (2016)
(defendant bears burden of proving affirmative defense by preponderance of the
evidence); State v. Mitchell, 166 N.H. 288, 291 (2014) (decision to admit or
exclude evidence is within discretion of trial court; we review trial court record to
determine whether objective basis sufficient to sustain discretionary rulings
exists).
Without a developed record, we are unable to discern whether the
challenges advanced on appeal were raised before the trial court and, if so, the
basis for the trial court rulings. Absent the relevant transcripts, we assume that
the record supports the evidentiary rulings made by the trial court. See Bean v.
Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250 (2004). We then examine the limited
record before us only for errors of law. State v. Woods, 139 N.H. 399, 403 (1995).
Having found none, we affirm the defendant’s conviction.
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Bassett, Donovan, and Countway, JJ., concurred.
