THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2026-0201, Caidan Berube v. David Chestnut,
the court on August 14, 2026, issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted on
appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See Sup. Ct.
R. 20(3). The defendant, David Chestnut, appeals an order of the Circuit Court
(Bedrick, J.), issued following a hearing, entering judgment in favor of the
plaintiff, Caidan Berube, in a small claims action for damages arising out of a
motor vehicle accident. On appeal, the defendant argues that the trial court
erred because he was not provided with certain exhibits in advance of the
hearing, and because the evidence was insufficient to support the trial court’s
order. However, “[i]t is the burden of the appealing party . . . to provide this court
with a record sufficient to decide [his] issues on appeal, as well as to demonstrate
that [he] raised [his] issues before the trial court.” Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt.,
151 N.H. 248, 250 (2004). Here, the defendant did not provide a transcript of the
hearing before the trial court. Nor did he provide a copy of a video of the accident
that was admitted as evidence. Thus, the defendant has neither demonstrated
that he preserved his arguments for our review, nor carried his burden to provide
us with a record sufficient to decide them. Indeed, without a transcript of the
hearing, “we must assume that the evidence was sufficient to support the result
reached by the trial court.” Id.; see also In the Matter of Birmingham &
Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51, 56-57 (2006) (noting that procedural rules are not
relaxed for self-represented parties). Accordingly, we affirm the trial court’s
decision.
Affirmed.
Donovan, Countway, Gould, and Will, JJ., concurred.
