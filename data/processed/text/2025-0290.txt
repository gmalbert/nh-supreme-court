THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2025-0290, H.T. v. D.D., the court on November
21, 2025, issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The plaintiff, H.T., appeals the order of the Circuit Court
(Clouatre, J.), following a hearing, denying her request for a civil stalking final
order of protection against the defendant, D.D. See RSA 633:3-a (2016). The
plaintiff argues that: (1) the defendant failed to comply with discovery
obligations and exceeded her allowed time at the hearing; (2) the trial court
erred in not allowing her to submit a video recording of her daughter’s
“testimony”; and (3) the defendant testified untruthfully at trial and continued
to stalk and harass the plaintiff’s daughter. We affirm.
The plaintiff first argues that the defendant failed to comply with
discovery obligations and “brought forth many claims and a witness[,] leaving
the plaintiff stunned with no time to prepare a defense.” The plaintiff also
asserts that the defendant exceeded her allowed time at the hearing, presenting
“numerous irrelevant claims . . . with no supporting evidence.” It is a long-
standing rule that parties may not have judicial review of matters not raised in
the trial court. Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250 (2004). It is
the burden of the appealing party, here the plaintiff, to provide this court with
a record sufficient to demonstrate that she raised her issues in the trial court.
Id.; see also In the Matter of Birmingham & Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51, 56
(2006) (self-represented litigants are bound by the same procedural rules that
govern parties represented by counsel). The purpose of this requirement is to
afford the trial court an opportunity to correct an error it may have made. In
the Matter of Mannion & Mannion, 155 N.H. 52, 54 (2007). In this case, the
record fails to show that the plaintiff raised these issues in the trial court.
Accordingly, we conclude that they are not preserved for review. See Bean, 151
N.H. at 250.
The plaintiff also argues that the trial court erred in not allowing her to
introduce a video recording of her daughter’s “testimony,” when the court later
allowed the defendant’s daughter to testify. The record shows that the court
denied the plaintiff’s request to allow the recording because the plaintiff’s
daughter was not present at the hearing. The court advised the plaintiff that
allowing the recording into evidence would deprive the defendant of her right to
cross-examine the witness. The plaintiff did not object to the court’s ruling.
See Milliken v. Dartmouth-Hitchcock Clinic, 154 N.H. 662, 665 (2006)
(Generally, a party must make a specific and contemporaneous objection to
preserve an issue for appellate review.). Nor did the plaintiff seek to have her
daughter testify at the second day of the hearing, when the defendant’s
daughter testified in person. Accordingly, we conclude that the plaintiff has
failed to preserve this issue for review. See Bean, 151 N.H. at 250.
The plaintiff also argues that the defendant testified untruthfully at trial,
and that the defendant continued to stalk and harass the plaintiff’s daughter
after the first day of the hearing. It is the role of the trial court to evaluate the
credibility of the witnesses and determine how much weight to give to the
witnesses’ testimony. In the Matter of Kurowski & Kurowski, 161 N.H. 578,
600 (2011). The trial court is not required to credit the testimony of any
witness. See In the Matter of Aube & Aube, 158 N.H. 459, 466 (2009). Our
role is to determine whether the trial court’s findings are reasonable given the
evidence presented at the hearing. State v. Moncada, 161 N.H. 791, 795
(2011). A person commits the offense of stalking if the person “[p]urposely,
knowingly, or recklessly engages in a course of conduct targeted at a specific
person which would cause a reasonable person to fear for his or her personal
safety. . . , and the person is actually placed in such fear.” RSA 633:3-a, I(a).
Based upon our review of the record, we conclude that the trial court
reasonably found that the evidence failed to show that the defendant stalked
the plaintiff. See id.
We need not address the plaintiff’s remaining issues as they were not
raised in the trial court, see Bean, 151 N.H. at 250, are not adequately briefed,
see State v. Blackmer, 149 N.H. 47, 48 (2003), and do not warrant further
discussion, see Vogel v. Vogel, 137 N.H. 321, 322 (1993).
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Donovan, Countway, and Gould, JJ., concurred.
