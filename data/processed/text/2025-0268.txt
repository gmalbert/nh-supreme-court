THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2025-0268, Farres Alkhayer v. Nashua Police
Department, the court on January 13, 2026, issued the following
order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The plaintiff, Farres Alkhayer, appeals the order of the
Circuit Court (Major, J.), following a hearing, denying his petition for a writ of
replevin seeking the return of his motor vehicle from the defendant, the Nashua
Police Department. See RSA ch. 536-A (2021). The plaintiff argues that the
defendant allegedly violated his rights to due process and equal protection by
towing his vehicle during a snow emergency. He also argues that the trial
court erred by: denying the petition; imposing an unreasonable bond
requirement; proceeding by offers of proof; failing to require proof of notice of,
or compliance with, snow emergency procedures; disregarding evidence of
hardship; and denying his motion for recusal. We affirm.
Following a hearing, the trial court found that the plaintiff’s unregistered
motor vehicle was parked on a public street during a snow emergency. Under
these circumstances, the court concluded, the defendant did not unlawfully
take possession of the vehicle by towing it. The court ruled that the plaintiff
may regain possession of the vehicle by following the city’s regular procedures.
It is the burden of the appealing party, here the plaintiff, to provide this
court with a record sufficient to decide his issues on appeal and to demonstrate
that he raised his issues in the trial court. See Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt.,
151 N.H. 248, 250 (2004); see also In the Matter of Birmingham &
Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51, 56 (2006) (self-represented litigants are bound by
the same procedural rules that govern parties represented by counsel). The
plaintiff has not provided us with a transcript of the April 25, 2025 hearing on
the merits. See Sup. Ct. R. 15(3) (“If the moving party intends to argue in the
supreme court that a finding or conclusion is unsupported by the evidence or
is contrary to the evidence, the moving party shall include in the record a
transcript of all evidence relevant to such finding or conclusion.”). In his notice
of appeal, the plaintiff affirmatively represented that a transcript was not
necessary. See Sup. Ct. R. 15(2)(a) (establishing procedure for ordering
transcripts in notice of appeal form). Each of the issues raised by the plaintiff
involves the trial court’s resolution of disputed factual issues. Absent a
transcript of the trial court hearing, we must assume that the evidence was
sufficient to support the decision reached. See Atwood v. Owens, 142 N.H.
396, 396 (1997). Accordingly, we review the trial court’s decision for errors of
law only, see id. at 397, and find none.
To the extent that the plaintiff argued in his motion to reconsider that
the trial court erred by conducting the hearing on offers of proof, we note that a
post-trial motion is generally insufficient to preserve an evidentiary objection
for appeal. See Broderick v. Watts, 136 N.H. 153, 168-69 (1992).
We also note that the plaintiff has failed to show that the trial judge erred
by not recusing himself in this matter. See Sup. Ct. R. 38, Canon 2.11; State
v. Bader, 148 N.H. 265, 268 (2002). Any remaining arguments in the plaintiff’s
brief are inadequately developed, see State v. Blackmer, 149 N.H. 47, 49
(2003), not preserved, see Bean, 151 N.H. at 250-51 (2004), and warrant no
further discussion, see Vogel v. Vogel, 137 N.H. 321, 322 (1993).
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Donovan, Countway, and Gould, JJ., concurred.
