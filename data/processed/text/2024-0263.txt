THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2024-0263, In the Matter of Estee Richards and
Robert Richards, the court on January 27, 2025, issued the
following order:
The respondent’s following motions are denied: (1) motion to seal
affidavit/to compel; (2) motion to unseal affidavit form; (3) motion for termination
of hearing/retrial/alimony allocation; (4) motion to unseal affidavit form; and
(5) motion to unseal affidavit form/termination of alimony/retrial. The
respondent’s objection to the petitioner’s brief will be treated as his reply brief.
The petitioner’s motion to strike medical records and motion to strike a
document are granted. On appeal, we consider only evidence and documents
presented to the trial court. Flaherty v. Dixey, 158 N.H. 385, 387 (2009); see
Sup. Ct. R. 13.
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted on
appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See Sup. Ct.
R. 20(2). The respondent, Robert B. Richards (husband), appeals the final decree
of the Circuit Court (Alfano, J.), following a hearing, in his divorce from the
petitioner, Estee Richards (wife). We construe the husband’s brief to argue that
the trial court erred in its distribution of the marital property and in its award of
alimony to the wife. We affirm.
The husband first argues that the trial court erred in its distribution of
marital property. We afford trial courts broad discretion in determining matters
of property distribution in fashioning a final divorce decree. In the Matter of
Hampers & Hampers, 154 N.H. 275, 285 (2006). Under RSA 458:16-a, II (2018),
an equal division of property is presumed equitable unless the trial court decides
otherwise after considering one or more of the factors designated in the statute.
In the Matter of Letendre & Letendre, 149 N.H. 31, 35 (2002). We will not
overturn the trial court’s decision absent an unsustainable exercise of discretion.
Hampers, 154 N.H. at 285. If the court’s findings can reasonably be made on the
evidence presented, they will stand. In the Matter of Peirano & Larsen, 155 N.H.
738, 749 (2007).
The trial court found the value of the marital home to be $400,000, which
is the value the husband’s expert placed on it. The court awarded the home to
the husband and awarded the wife half the equity, $200,000. The court awarded
each party the bank accounts in his or her own name and the motor vehicle in
her or her possession. The court approved the parties’ agreement on the division
of personal property. We have considered the husband’s challenges to the trial
court’s decisions regarding the marital property. Based upon this record, we
cannot conclude that the trial court unsustainably exercised its discretion in its
property distribution. See id.
The husband next argues that the trial court erred in its alimony award.
The trial court is afforded broad discretion in awarding alimony. In the Matter of
Nassar & Nassar, 156 N.H. 769, 772 (2008). We will not overturn its decision
absent an unsustainable exercise of discretion. Id.
The parties agreed that the husband would pay the wife monthly alimony
for approximately 21 years. The trial court found that the amount of alimony
pursuant to the formula set forth in RSA 458:19-a, II(a) (Supp. 2023) would be
$2,255 per month, and that justice required an adjustment to $3,500 per month
based upon the husband’s ability to pay, the wife’s needs, and the parties’ health
issues. See RSA 458:19-a, II(a), IV (Supp. 2023). We have considered the
husband’s challenges to the trial court’s decisions regarding alimony. Based
upon this record, we cannot conclude that the trial court unsustainably
exercised its discretion in its alimony award. See Nassar, 156 N.H. at 772.
To the extent that the husband’s brief may be construed to raise other
arguments, we conclude that they are undeveloped and warrant no further
consideration. See State v. Blackmer, 149 N.H. 47, 49 (2003).
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Bassett, Donovan, and Countway, JJ., concurred.
