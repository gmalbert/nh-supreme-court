THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2026-0094, M.C. v. R.B., the court on June 8,
2026, issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The defendant, R.B., appeals the order of the Circuit Court
(Chabot, J.), denying his motion to vacate a final domestic violence order for
the protection of the plaintiff, M.C., based upon newly discovered evidence. We
construe the defendant’s brief to argue that the trial court erred in denying his
motion without a hearing. We affirm.
To obtain a new trial based upon newly discovered evidence, the
defendant must establish that: (1) he was not at fault for failing to discover the
evidence prior to trial; (2) the evidence is admissible, material to the merits,
and not cumulative; and (3) the evidence is of such a character that a different
result will probably be reached upon a new trial. State v. Etienne, 163 N.H.
57, 96 (2011); Rautenberg v. Munnis, 109 N.H. 25, 26 (1968). A “single
exception” to the requirement that the moving party show a likelihood of a
different result is when a party gives false and dishonest testimony concerning
a material issue. Barton v. Plaisted, 109 N.H. 428, 432 (1969). This exception
applies when the testimony is “of such a dishonest nature as to strongly
suggest an attempt to perpetrate a fraud on the trial court.” Sylvain v. Estate
of Sylvain, 117 N.H. 546, 548-49 (1977).
“Whether newly discovered evidence requires a new trial is a question of
fact for the trial court.” State v. Breest, 169 N.H. 640, 653 (2017) (quotation
omitted). “We will sustain the trial court’s decision unless its conclusion is
clearly unreasonable.” Id. (quotation omitted); see Rautenberg, 109 N.H. at 26.
In this case, the defendant alleged in his motion that he obtained newly
discovered evidence, including “official transcripts and sworn records that were
not available at the time of the hearing,” which “demonstrate repeated, material
inconsistences in the Plaintiff’s sworn testimony.” The defendant provided little
or no additional information to show that: (1) he was not at fault for not
discovering the evidence prior to the hearing; (2) the evidence is admissible,
material to the merits, and not cumulative; and (3) the evidence is of such a
character that a different result will probably be reached at a new hearing. See
Etienne, 163 N.H. at 96. Nor did the defendant allege that the plaintiff’s
testimony was “of such a dishonest nature as to strongly suggest an attempt to
perpetrate a fraud on the trial court.” Sylvain, 117 N.H. at 548-49. Rather, the
defendant sought “leave to present [his documents] at a limited evidentiary
hearing.”
The trial court denied the motion without a hearing, noting that we
affirmed its findings on appeal, see M.C. v. R.B., No. 2025-0377 (non-
precedential order), 2025 WL 3512722 (N.H. Nov. 20, 2025), and that there was
“no basis to reopen the case.” We review the trial court’s decision to rule on
the motion without a hearing for an unsustainable exercise of discretion. See
In re Erik M., 146 N.H. 508, 511 (2001); State v. Lambert, 147 N.H. 295, 296
(2001) (explaining unsustainable exercise of discretion standard). “To reopen a
case just because a party persists in asserting and attempting to prove that his
version of the dispute was the truth and that of the opponent false would keep
the dispute going without end.” Bricker v. Sceva Speare Hosp., 115 N.H. 709,
711 (1975). Based upon this record, we cannot conclude that the trial court
unsustainably exercised its discretion in denying the motion without a hearing.
See Erik M., 146 N.H. at 511; Breest, 169 N.H. at 653.
We need not address the defendant’s remaining arguments as they were
not raised in the trial court, see Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248,
250 (2004), are not adequately briefed, see State v. Blackmer, 149 N.H. 47, 48
(2003), and do not warrant further discussion, see Vogel v. Vogel, 137 N.H.
321, 322 (1993).
Affirmed.
Donovan, Countway, Gould, and Will, JJ., concurred.
