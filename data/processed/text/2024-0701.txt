THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2024-0701, In the Matter of Anthony Lee and
Jade Lee, the court on November 24, 2025, issued the following
order:
The petitioner’s motion to expedite appeal is deemed moot, in light of this
order. The petitioner’s motions to compel disclosure of medical and school
records, to compel production of medical, school, and tax documentation, and
for immediate enforcement of prior orders are denied without prejudice to
seeking such relief in the trial court.
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The petitioner, Anthony Lee (husband), appeals the final
decree of the Circuit Court (Paine, J.), following a hearing, in his divorce from
the respondent, Jade Lee (wife). The husband challenges the trial court’s
decisions (1) granting primary residential responsibility and sole decision-
making responsibility for the parties’ three children to the wife, (2) approving
the wife’s relocation with the children to Virginia, (3) ordering him to pay child
support, and (4) dividing the marital property. We construe the husband’s
brief to argue that the trial court’s decisions were not supported by the
evidence and were contrary to the weight of the evidence. We affirm.
We review the trial court’s decisions regarding these matters under our
unsustainable exercise of discretion standard. See In the Matter of Kurowski &
Kurowski, 161 N.H. 578, 585 (2011) (parenting rights and responsibilities); In
the Matter of Pfeuffer & Pfeuffer, 150 N.H. 257, 259 (2003) (relocation); In the
Matter of Hennessey-Martin & Whitney, 151 N.H. 207, 212 (2004) (child
support); In the Matter of Hampers & Hampers, 154 N.H. 275, 285 (2006)
(property division). Under this standard, we consider only whether the record
establishes an objective basis sufficient to sustain the trial court’s
discretionary judgment, and we will not disturb the court’s decisions if they
could reasonably have been made. In the Matter of Kurowski, 161 N.H. at 585.
We will affirm the trial court’s findings and rulings unless they are
unsupported by the evidence or erroneous as a matter of law. In the Matter of
Nyhan and Nyhan, 147 N.H. 768, 770 (2002).
In its final orders, the trial court granted primary residential
responsibility and sole decision-making responsibility for the parties’ three
children to the wife and approved her relocation with the children to Virginia
on a permanent basis. The court ordered the husband to pay the guideline-
level of child support, $1,189 monthly. The court awarded the husband one
half of the wife’s TIAA CREF retirement plan and awarded the wife one half of
the husband’s retirement assets pursuant to the Hodgins formula. See
Hodgins v. Hodgins, 126 N.H. 711, 715 (1985). The court ordered the equity in
the marital home to be divided, awarding 60 percent to the husband and 40
percent to wife.
At the outset, we note that the husband’s brief lacks well-developed legal
arguments. See State v. Blackmer, 149 N.H. 47, 49 (2003) (noting that
complaints regarding adverse rulings without developed legal arguments are
insufficient to warrant judicial review); see also In the Matter of Birmingham &
Birmingham, 154 N.H. 51, 56 (2006) (self-represented litigants are bound by
the same procedural rules that govern parties represented by counsel). The
husband argues generally that the trial court erroneously ignored certain
evidence that was unfavorable to the wife and ignored other evidence that was
favorable to him. He also argues that the court’s findings favorable to the wife
and unfavorable to him were unsupported by the evidence and contrary to the
weight of the evidence. He further argues that the court erred in relying upon
the testimony of the guardian ad litem because she allegedly was unqualified,
negligent, and biased against him.
“It is not our role to calculate how much weight the trial court should
afford specific evidence, second guess its decisions regarding witness
credibility, or substitute our judgment for that of the trial court on a
discretionary ruling.” In the Matter of Kurowski, 161 N.H. at 600. The fact
finder may accept or reject, in whole or in part, the testimony of any witness or
party, and is not required to believe even uncontroverted evidence. In the
Matter of Aube & Aube, 158 N.H. 459, 466 (2009). We conclude that the
record provides an objective basis sufficient to support the trial court’s
decisions. See In the Matter of Kurowski, 161 N.H. at 600. We reject the
husband’s assertion that the trial court was biased against him. See Sup. Ct.
R. 38, Canon 2.11; State v. Bader, 148 N.H. 265, 271 (2002) (noting that
adverse judicial rulings alone do not render a judge biased).
To the extent that the husband raises additional issues, we conclude
that they are inadequately briefed, see State v. Blackmer, 149 N.H. at 49, not
preserved, see Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250-51 (2004), or
beyond the scope of this appeal.
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Donovan and Gould, JJ., concurred.
