THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2024-0681, Francis A. Delorier, Jr. v. Maine
Health & a., the court on November 6, 2025, issued the following
order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The plaintiff, Francis A. Delorier, Jr., appeals an order of the
Superior Court (Attorri, J.) granting summary judgment in favor of the
defendants, Victor Lazaron, Maine Health, and Memorial Hospital, on his
complaint seeking an award of damages based upon Lazaron’s alleged
negligence in the performance of a surgical procedure. In granting summary
judgment, the trial court determined that the plaintiff had not properly
disclosed sufficient expert testimony to meet his burden of proof under RSA
507-E:2 (2010). The plaintiff argues that, in so ruling, the trial court
erroneously construed RSA 516:29-b (2021), and that, as a matter of law, his
expert witness disclosure satisfied RSA 516:29-b. We affirm.
We review the trial court’s ruling on summary judgment by considering the
summary judgment record in the light most favorable to the non-moving party.
New Hampshire Health Care Assoc. v. Governor, 161 N.H. 378, 384 (2011). We
will affirm if the record does not reveal any genuine issues of material fact, that
is, facts that would affect the outcome of the case, and if the moving party is
entitled to judgment as a matter of law. Id. A defendant is entitled to summary
judgment if the record establishes that the plaintiff will be unable to carry his or
her burden of proof at trial. See Long v. Long, 136 N.H. 25, 32 (1992).
As the appealing party, the plaintiff has the burden of demonstrating
reversible error. Gallo v. Traina, 166 N.H. 737, 740 (2014). Based upon our
review of the trial court’s order, the plaintiff’s arguments, the relevant law, and
the summary judgment record viewed in the light most favorable to the
plaintiff, we conclude that the plaintiff did not demonstrate a genuine issue of
material fact below, or that the defendants are not entitled to judgment as a
matter of law. See id.; New Hampshire Health Care Assoc., 161 N.H. at 384. In
light of this order, the plaintiff’s motion to expedite the appeal is moot.
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Donovan, Countway, and Gould, JJ., concurred.
