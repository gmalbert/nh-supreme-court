THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2024-0594, Aldean Khater v. New Hampshire
Department of Revenue Administration, the court on July 18,
2025, issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The plaintiff, Aldean Khater, appeals the order of the
Superior Court (Kissinger, J.), granting the motion for judgment on the
pleadings filed by the New Hampshire Department of Revenue Administration
(DRA) and dismissing the plaintiff’s appeal of a DRA administrative order.
Concluding that the superior court lacked subject matter jurisdiction to review
the DRA order, we affirm.
On May 10, 2022, the DRA issued a final order in an administrative
proceeding relating to the plaintiff’s business taxes for tax years 2010 through
2015 and his tobacco taxes for tax periods from May of 2013 through
December of 2016. In the May 10, 2022 letter enclosing the order, the DRA
hearing officer stated, “[a]s provided in RSA 21-J:28-b, IV, you have the right to
appeal this order within (30) days to . . . the Superior Court of the county in
which you reside.” On May 26, 2022, the plaintiff filed his appeal in the
Middlesex County Superior Court in Massachusetts because, he alleges, he
resides in Middlesex County, Massachusetts. On March 7, 2023, the
Massachusetts court dismissed his appeal for lack of subject matter
jurisdiction, ruling that it lacked jurisdiction over the DRA. On March 28,
2023, the plaintiff filed his appeal in the Hillsborough County Superior Court,
Southern Division.
On August 21, 2024, the DRA filed a motion for judgment on the
pleadings arguing, among other things, that the superior court lacked subject
matter jurisdiction due to the plaintiff’s untimely filing of the appeal. On
October 7, 2024, the trial court granted DRA’s motion and dismissed the case.
The procedures in RSA 21-J:28-a (2020 & Supp. 2023) and RSA 21-J:28-
b (2020) provide the “exclusive method by which taxpayers may challenge their
liability for any tax, or the application to them of any provision” of the statutes
governing the DRA. RSA 21-J:28-a, VI; Eby v. State, 166 N.H. 321, 343 (2014).
The DRA issued the plaintiff a notice of decision regarding its tax assessment
on May 10, 2022. RSA 21-J:28-b, IV provides that, “[w]ithin 30 days of the
notice of decision, the taxpayer may appeal such decision by written
application to the board of tax and land appeals or the superior court, in the
county in which the taxpayer resides or has a place of business or resident
agent.” To comply with RSA 21-J:28-b, IV, the plaintiff was required to file his
appeal on or before June 9, 2022. The plaintiff filed his appeal on March 29,
2023, more than nine months after the deadline.
Strictly complying with a statutory appeal deadline is generally necessary
to vest the superior court with subject matter jurisdiction in an appeal from an
administrative agency decision. See Dermody v. Town of Gilford, 137 N.H. 294,
296 (1993); see also Schwartz v. State, 135 N.H. 470, 473 (1992) (decided
under prior law). If an appeal is filed after the expiration of the thirty-day
period, subject matter jurisdiction does not vest. See Appeal of Carreau, 157
N.H. 122, 123 (2008) (dismissing for lack of jurisdiction an appeal brought
under RSA chapter 541 filed one day late). We conclude that the superior
court lacked subject matter jurisdiction to consider the plaintiff’s appeal. See
id. Accordingly, we affirm its order granting the DRA’s motion for judgment on
the pleadings.
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Donovan and Countway, JJ., concurred.
