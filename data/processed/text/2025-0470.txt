THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2025-0470, Joseph S. Haas, Jr. v. School
Administrative Unit/SAU79 & a., the court on June 22, 2026,
issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted on
appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See Sup. Ct.
R. 20(3). The plaintiff, Joseph S. Haas, Jr., appeals orders of the Superior Court
(Leonard, J.): (1) dismissing his complaint against the defendants, School
Administrative Unit # 79 and the Town of Gilmanton, on the basis that the
complaint failed to state a claim upon which relief may be granted; and (2)
awarding the town attorney’s fees. The plaintiff challenges these rulings and
argues that the trial judge should have disqualified herself. We affirm.
When reviewing the trial court’s dismissal order, we consider whether the
allegations in the plaintiff’s complaint are reasonably susceptible of a
construction that would permit recovery. See Szewczyk v. Continental Paving,
176 N.H. 148, 151 (2023). We assume the truth of the well-pleaded factual
allegations in the complaint and construe all reasonable inferences from those
facts in the light most favorable to the plaintiff. See id. We do not, however,
assume the truth of allegations that “are not well-pleaded, including the
statement of conclusions of fact and principles of law.” ERG, Inc. v. Barnes, 137
N.H. 186, 190 (1993). We will uphold the trial court’s decision if the facts
pleaded do not constitute a basis for relief. See Szewczyk, 176 N.H. at 151.
As the appealing party, the plaintiff has the burden of demonstrating
reversible error. Gallo v. Traina, 166 N.H. 737, 740 (2014). It is also the
plaintiff’s burden to provide those portions of the trial court record that are
necessary to decide the issues he raises on appeal, and we assume that relevant
portions of the trial court record not provided on appeal support the trial court’s
decision. See Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250 (2004). This
requirement applies to relevant trial court pleadings. See Estate of Day v.
Hanover Ins. Co. 162 N.H. 415, 422 (2011).
In this case, the plaintiff has not provided a copy of his complaint. Nor has
he provided a copy of the town’s motion for attorney’s fees or the exhibit to the
motion referenced in the trial court’s order granting the motion.
Based upon our review of the trial court’s well-reasoned orders, the plaintiff’s
arguments, the relevant law, and the portions of the record provided on appeal,
we conclude that the plaintiff has failed to demonstrate that the trial court erred
by dismissing his complaint or awarding the town attorney’s fees, or to establish
a basis for the trial judge’s disqualification. See id.; Szewczyk, 176 N.H. at 151;
see also Sup. Ct. R. 25(8).
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Donovan, Countway, Gould, and Will, JJ.,
concurred.
