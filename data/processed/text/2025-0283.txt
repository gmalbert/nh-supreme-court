THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2025-0283, Appeal of Oscar Cordon Peralta, the
court on March 4, 2026, issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The claimant, Oscar Cordon Peralta, appeals a decision of
the New Hampshire Compensation Appeals Board (CAB) that denied his
requests for: 1) compensation for certain medical bills pursuant to RSA 281-
A:23 (2023); and 2) reinstatement of temporary total disability benefits
pursuant to RSA 281-A:48 (2023). For the reasons set forth below, we affirm in
part, vacate in part, and remand.
On January 28, 2022, while completing a drywall job performed for
Semper Drywall (Employer), Claimant fell 30 feet from scaffolding and suffered
numerous injuries, including an intraparenchymal hemorrhage, subdural
hematoma, multiple facial fractures, and scalp and forehead lacerations.
Claimant sought worker’s compensation benefits. Because Employer did not
carry workers’ compensation insurance, Claimant petitioned the New
Hampshire Department of Labor (DOL) to declare an employer-employee
relationship and entitlement to benefits. Before the DOL hearing officer,
Employer alleged that benefits were unavailable because the injury was caused
by the fault of Claimant.
On November 28, 2022, the DOL held a hearing, and on December 19,
2022, it issued an order finding that Claimant was an employee, was not at
fault for the injury, and was entitled to temporary total disability benefits. The
DOL found that following the accident, Claimant was “essentially bed ridden
with multiple injuries for a few months,” and that Claimant “testified credibly
and the medical records support that his injuries sustained on January 28,
2022 have kept him out of work since the date of injury.” Finally, the DOL
ruled that:
As causation was the threshold issue and all denials were based
on causation, there is no finding on RSA 281-A:23 at this time. In
accordance with this decision the medical bills shall be re-
evaluated and accepted and/or denied in accordance with the law.
Employer appealed to the CAB, which held a hearing on May 10, 2023,
approximately six months after the DOL hearing.
A significant focus of the May 10, 2023 CAB hearing was Claimant’s
physical condition as of that date, and the panel’s discussion concentrated
particularly on a private investigator’s video footage from April 25, 2023 — i.e.,
approximately 14 months after the injury — that supported a finding that
Claimant had regained work capacity. Ultimately, the CAB ruled consistently
with the DOL that Claimant was an employee of Employer, that he was injured
at work, and that he was not at fault for the injury. However, the CAB also
stated that “[t]he question facing the Panel is whether and to what extent he
remained disabled as of November 28, 2022, the date of the [DOL] hearing.”
The CAB then determined, based on an approximately four-minute clip derived
from the April 25, 2023 video, that Claimant had full-time, full-duty work
capacity for drywall taping on November 28, 2022, the date of the DOL hearing,
and it terminated Claimant’s weekly indemnity payments.
As to payment of previously incurred medical costs pursuant to RSA
281-A:23, the 2023 CAB panel noted that:
The [DOL]’s December 19, 2022 [order] held that “[a]s causation
was the threshold issue and all denials were based on causation,
there is no finding on RSA 281-A:23 at this time. . . .” There were
no denied medical bills in evidence at the [CAB] hearing. The
Panel can only address medical bills properly before it and leaves
this issue to be addressed by the [DOL] in the first instance should
adjudication become necessary.
Neither party appealed the CAB’s May 23, 2023 decision to this court.
Thereafter, Employer denied payment for any medical treatment
occurring after February 17, 2022, i.e., approximately 3 weeks after the injury,
because this was the date of the latest medical records submitted to the DOL
for the November 28, 2022 hearing. Claimant asserts that it took until June
29, 2022 for him to have a follow-up neurology appointment, and, at the time
of his initial DOL hearing, his counsel had been unable to obtain copies of the
relevant post-February 2022 medical records, despite attempts. Claimant’s
neurologist, Elena Ivashina, ordered a follow-up MRI of Claimant’s brain that
showed “residual post injury changes, sequela of probable prior intracranial
hemorrhages and small left frontal lobe gliosis.” Ivashina referred Claimant to
physical therapy and speech therapy.
As a result of Employer’s denial of payment of Claimant’s post-February
2022 medical bills, Claimant petitioned the DOL seeking payment and
reinstatement of temporary total disability benefits. On September 3, 2024,
the DOL held a hearing. Claimant raised two independent statutory grounds
for reinstatement of benefits pursuant to RSA 281-A:48, I: (1) either that he
had undergone a “change in condition” since the last assessment of the extent
2
of his disability; or (2) that there had been a “mistake as to the nature or extent
of the injury or disability” due to the lack of medical records for review.
Through its October 16, 2024 order, the DOL addressed the records related to
Claimant’s neurology treatment with Ivashina, and it determined that
“Claimant did not meet his burden of proving that the treatments on and after
June 29, 2022 were causally related to the injury on the [date of injury],
reasonable, or medically necessary.” The DOL further found that Claimant
“failed to prove a change in conditions warranting reinstatement of indemnity.”
Thereafter, Claimant appealed to the CAB. The CAB similarly found that
Claimant had failed to demonstrate a change of condition and denied both the
payment of medical bills and the reinstatement of indemnity benefits. The CAB
did not address whether there had been a mistake as to the nature or extent of
the injury under RSA 281-A:48 that would justify reinstating benefits. The
CAB also did not clearly address the RSA 281-A:23 question of whether the
medical treatment Claimant received from June 29, 2022 was reasonably
related to the January 28, 2022 injury.
On appeal Claimant argues that the CAB erred because it conflated the
RSA 281-A:48 change in condition analysis (related to the reinstatement of
indemnity benefits) with the RSA 281-A:23 assessment of the causal
relationship between his post-February 2022 medical treatment and the
compensable injury, thus applying an incorrect standard to his RSA 281-A:23
request. Claimant further asserts that the CAB erred by failing to address his
arguments regarding “mistake as to the nature or extent of the injury or
disability” relative to the RSA 281-A:48 analysis. We agree with Claimant in
both regards.
We will not disturb the CAB’s decision absent an error of law, or unless,
by a clear preponderance of the evidence, we find it to be unjust or
unreasonable. Appeal of Doody, 172 N.H. 802, 805 (2020); see RSA 541:13
(2021). The CAB’s factual findings are prima facie lawful and reasonable.
Appeal of Estate of Menke, 177 N.H. 241, 244-45 (2025). We review the CAB’s
factual findings deferentially and will uphold them unless the evidence does
not support them. Id. We review the CAB’s legal rulings de novo. Id.

incurred after February 17, 2022, we conclude that the CAB erred, because its
analysis incorrectly focused on whether there had been a change in Claimant’s
condition. RSA 281-A:23, I, provides: “An employer subject to this chapter . . .

medical, surgical, and hospital services, remedial care, nursing, medicines, and
mechanical and surgical aids for such period as the nature of the injury may
require.” Id. When evaluating a dispute regarding an RSA 281-A:23 payment
