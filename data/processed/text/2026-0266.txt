THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2026-0266, Zac Correia v. Kimberly Brandolini,
the court on August 14, 2026, issued the following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The defendant, Kimberly Brandolini (landlord), appeals an
order of the Circuit Court (Lyons, R., approved by Spony, J.), following a
hearing, awarding the plaintiff, Zac Correia (tenant), statutory damages of
$1,000 based upon the defendant’s willful violation of her obligation to
investigate and remediate the tenant’s report of a pest infestation. See RSA
540-A:3, V-a, :4, IX(a) (2021). The landlord argues that the trial court erred by
disregarding her testimony concerning her efforts to investigate and remediate
the infestation, and by making findings of fact inconsistent with her testimony.
The landlord further argues that the trial court erred by awarding statutory
damages because, under RSA 540-A:4, IX(d) (Supp. 2025), failures to
investigate and remediate pest infestations are expressly excluded from a
statutory damages award. We affirm in part, vacate in part, and remand.
In an appeal from an award of damages under RSA 540-A:4, IX(a), the
trial court’s “findings of fact shall be final but questions of law may be
transferred to the supreme court in the same manner as from the superior
court.” RSA 540-A:4, V (2021). “By the statute’s plain terms, review is limited
and, as to factual findings, it is highly deferential.” Magee v. Cooper, 174 N.H.
647, 650 (2021). We will uphold the trial court’s findings and rulings unless
they lack evidentiary support or are otherwise tainted by error of law. Id. at
651. “Our inquiry is to determine whether the evidence presented to the trial
court reasonably supports its findings, and then whether the court’s decision is
consonant with applicable law.” Id. (quotation and brackets omitted). We defer
to the trial court’s evaluation of the credibility of the witnesses and the weight
of the evidence presented. See Cook v. Sullivan, 149 N.H. 774, 780 (2003).
It is the burden of the appealing party, here, the landlord, to provide a
record on appeal that is sufficient to decide the issues she is raising. See Bean
v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151 N.H. 248, 250 (2004). In the absence of a
transcript of an evidentiary hearing, we will assume that a transcript would
support the result reached by the trial court. See id.
RSA 540-A:3, V-a provides:
No landlord shall willfully fail to investigate a tenant’s report
of an infestation of insects, including bed bugs, or rodents in the
tenant’s rented or leased premises, within 7 days of receiving
notice of such alleged infestation from the tenant or a municipal
health or housing code authority, or fail to take reasonable
measures to remediate an infestation.
Under RSA 540-A:4, IX(a), “[a]ny landlord . . . who violates . . . any provision of
RSA 540-A:3 shall be subject to the civil remedies set forth in RSA 358-A:10 for
the initial violation . . . . Each day that a violation continues after issuance of
a temporary order shall constitute a separate violation.” In turn, RSA 358-A:10
(2022) generally entitles the tenant who has prevailed on a claim against a
landlord for a violation of a “provision of RSA 540-A:3” to the statutory remedy
of $1,000 plus costs and attorney’s fees without having to plead or prove actual
damages. See Carter v. Lachance, 146 N.H. 11, 14 (2001). RSA 540-A:4, IX(d),
however, expressly limits this statutory remedy by stating that “[t]he provisions
of subparagraph (a) shall not apply to any violation of 540-A:3, V-a.”
In this case, the trial court found that the landlord willfully failed “to
investigate and remediate a pest infestation in the apartment” leased by the
tenant, and that she “did nothing to comply” with the trial court’s March 6,
2026 temporary order “[d]espite [her] claim that she had retained 3 separate
firms to address the pest issue as far back as December 2025.” On these
findings, the court awarded “statutory damages” of $1,000 for the landlord’s
willful violation of RSA 540-A:3, V-a. Although the landlord challenges the trial
court’s findings that she willfully failed to investigate and remediate the
infestation, and that she did nothing to comply with the temporary order, she
has failed to provide a transcript of the evidentiary hearing. Accordingly, we
assume that the trial court’s findings are supported by the evidence, and we
affirm them. See Bean, 151 N.H. at 250.
Nevertheless, the plain language of RSA 540-A:4, IX(d) expressly excludes
violations of RSA 540-A:3, V-a from the statutory remedy of RSA 540-A:4, IX(a).
Under these circumstances, we vacate the trial court’s award of $1,000 in
statutory damages under RSA 540-A:4, IX(a), and remand for further
proceedings consistent with this order.
Affirmed in part; reversed in
part; and remanded.
Donovan, Countway, Gould, and Will, JJ., concurred.
