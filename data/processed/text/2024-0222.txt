THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2024-0222, SCJ Commercial Financial Services
v. Brian Clark & a., the court on March 27, 2025, issued the
following order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The defendants, Brian Clark and Granite State Heavy
Hauling, LLC, appeal the order of the Superior Court (Kissinger, J.), granting
summary judgment in favor of the plaintiff, SCJ Commercial Financial
Services, on its claim for damages pursuant to a secured finance agreement
and a personal guaranty. We affirm.
The defendants argue that the trial court erred in finding no genuine
issue of material fact as to whether: (1) the plaintiff had standing to sue, when
the plaintiff was not an original party to the agreement; (2) the plaintiff
correctly calculated the amount of the deficiency balance; (3) defendant Clark
had a duty to read the agreement and personal guaranty, despite his inability
to read the documents on a cell phone while traveling, and despite the lender’s
failure to explain that he was signing a personal guaranty; and (4) the plaintiff
acted in a commercially reasonable manner. The defendants also argue that
the plaintiff was not entitled to judgment as a matter of law. See RSA 491:8-a
(2010).
As the appealing parties, the defendants have the burden of
demonstrating reversible error. Gallo v. Traina, 166 N.H. 737, 740 (2014).
Based upon our review of the trial court’s well-reasoned order, the defendants’
challenges to it, the relevant law, and the record submitted on appeal, we
conclude that the defendants have not demonstrated reversible error. See id.
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Bassett, Donovan, and Countway, JJ., concurred.
