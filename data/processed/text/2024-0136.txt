THE STATE OF NEW HAMPSHIRE
SUPREME COURT
In Case No. 2024-0136, State of New Hampshire v. Marc J.
Manchon, the court on April 15, 2025, issued the following
order:
The court has reviewed the written arguments and the record submitted
on appeal, and has determined to resolve the case by way of this order. See
Sup. Ct. R. 20(3). The defendant, Marc J. Manchon, appeals his conviction,
following a bench trial in the Circuit Court (McIntyre, J.), on a charge of
resisting arrest or detention occurring within the context of an investigation for
violating a criminal bail protective order. See RSA 642:2 (2016). He argues
that: (1) his arrest was unlawful under the State Constitution because the
arresting officers had not first verified the existence of the protective order; (2)
the charge of resisting arrest or detention, which, the defendant asserts,
“replaced” an earlier charge of disobeying a police officer, was allegedly
inconsistent and arbitrary, and was allegedly not based upon a lawful arrest or
detention, in violation of due process under the State Constitution; (3) his
arrest was allegedly in retaliation for his exercise of protected speech in
violation of the State Constitution; (4) the State’s alleged “failure to follow
proper procedure raises serious due process concerns under Part I, Article 15
of the New Hampshire Constitution”; and (5) the trial court’s failure to
articulate specific reasons for its finding of guilt violated his state
constitutional right to due process. We affirm.
It is the defendant’s burden on appeal to establish that the arguments he
raises were raised in the trial court. See Bean v. Red Oak Prop. Mgmt., 151
N.H. 248, 250 (2004); State v. Blackmer, 149 N.H. 47, 48 (2003). We generally
will not address arguments that were not preserved in the trial court. See
Blackmer, 149 N.H. at 48. Nor will we address arguments that are not
supported by developed legal argument. See id. at 49. We have reviewed each
of the arguments raised by the defendant in his brief and memorandum of law,
and we conclude that the arguments either were not raised in the trial court,
see id. at 48, are not sufficiently developed, see id. at 49, or otherwise do not
warrant further discussion, see Vogel v. Vogel, 137 N.H. 321, 322 (1993).
Affirmed.
MacDonald, C.J., and Bassett, Donovan, and Countway, JJ., concurred.
